Test significance across processes with --runs

--pvalue ran Welch's t-test over the measured iterations of one process
per executable. Iterations of one process share its heap layout, GC
timing, hash seed and JIT decisions, so they are not independent
samples: the test sees only the noise inside a process, while two
processes of the same Ruby differ by more than that. On an A/A run of
ten identical processes per benchmark (YJIT and the interpreter,
railsbench, lobsters, fib, respond_to, str_concat), 214 of 450 pairs of
identical builds were marked significant at p < 0.05, where 5% is
expected.

--runs N now runs each benchmark in N processes per executable,
interleaved, alternating the executable order every round, and the
t-test compares the N per-process means. On the same processes, 5
against 5 gave 103 significant results out of 2,000 random groupings
(5%). End to end, four identical Rubies with --runs 5 gave 2 of 30
significant comparisons, while the single-process test called 566 of
the 950 pairs among those same 100 processes significant.

The default stays at one process, so runs that don't ask for p-values
take no longer. With one process the p-value is N/A and --pvalue warns,
rather than showing stars it can't support. The timing columns report
all measured iterations of the N processes, so their stddev includes
the variation between processes, and the 1st itr ratio averages the
first iteration of each process. The JSON keeps the concatenated
series and records the iterations of each process in
warmup_itrs_per_run and bench_itrs_per_run; other values, such as JIT
stats, come from the first process. --runs refuses the Ractor harness
for now, because its per-Ractor-count results are not merged across
processes.
